# Voice

How Batchlabel copy is written. If a change to the site reads like a brochure, it is wrong.

## The rules

Plain British English. Warm, unfussy, a knowledgeable person explaining something to a
customer.

- **Short sentences.** Vary the length, default short. If a sentence needs a comma to
  rescue it, split it.
- **Name a real thing.** A document, a file format, a regulation, a number, a minute
  count. "Upload the safety data sheet your fragrance supplier gave you", not "leverage
  your supplier documentation".
- **Say it once.** No restating the heading in the sentence below it. No saying the same
  idea again in different words.
- **One adjective beats three.**
- **Headlines say what the thing is or does.** No wordplay that costs clarity.
- **Never claim what is not built.** No dates, no "coming soon", no invented popularity.
  See POSITIONING.md.
- Where the copy is already plain and good, leave it alone.

## Banned

Hollow antithesis — "It's not just X, it's Y", "No X. Just Y", "Not because X, but
because Y". "Whether you're..." openers. Rule-of-three padding — "fast, simple and
compliant". Rhetorical questions as headings. Fake conversational filler — "Here's the
thing", "We get it", "Sound familiar?". Parallel bullet lists where every item has the
same shape and padding. Em dashes and colons in headlines. Emoji in headings.

Words: seamless, effortless, unlock, empower, elevate, streamline, robust, leverage,
journey, landscape, "in today's world", "peace of mind", "game-changer", "designed to",
"crafted", "built to help you", "take the guesswork out", "say goodbye to", "simply",
"easily", "we've got you covered", "dive in", "at its core", "the beauty of".

## Before and after

**Hero subhead** — the claim was not true.
> The same engine that gets candle labels right is built to extend across categories. Candles and home fragrance today, cosmetics labelling coming next.

> Candles and home fragrance is the only category we cover today. We would like to add more. None of them are built yet, and we will not pretend otherwise.

**Hero body** — rule of three, doing no work.
> ...and download a print ready label. No consultant, no spreadsheet, no guesswork.

> ...then download a print ready label.

**Homepage categories** — abstraction where a fact belongs.
> The hard part is never the artwork, it is turning safety and regulatory data into the exact words the rules require. That engine is the same whatever you make, so we are opening it up one category at a time, starting where we started, with candles.

> Candles and home fragrance is the only category you can label with Batchlabel today. The rest of this is where we would like to go.

**"Everything that goes on the label"** — the lead restated the heading.
> One label, built from your safety data sheet and your recipe. Here is what appears on it.

> Every line comes from your safety data sheet or from your recipe.

**How it works, intro** — colon, abstraction, a "No X needed" that says nothing. The last
sentence was already the best one, so it is the only one left.
> The same three steps work for any category we take on: turn the data that governs a product into a compliant label. Below, we walk it through with candles, where we started. No compliance knowledge needed. If you can read the safety data sheet your supplier emailed you, you can make a label.

> Three steps, walked through with a candle. If you can read the safety data sheet your supplier emailed you, you can make a label.

**FAQ, "Do you do cosmetics labelling?"** — said yes-ish while meaning no.
> Not yet, but it is the next category we are building, with makers who already use us for their candle labels. It will take ingredient and product information and produce a compliant cosmetics label, with the allergen and INCI wording the rules expect.

> No. It is the category makers ask us for most and the one we would most like to add, but we have not started building it and there is no date.

**About, intro** — "mission", a colon, and a sharpest pain.
> Batchlabel is a small UK company on a straightforward mission: take the compliance labelling that trips up small makers and make it quick and correct. We started with candles because that is where the pain was sharpest. We are not a compliance firm and we are not pretending to be one.

> We are a small UK company. We make the labelling part of selling candles quicker to get right. We are not a compliance firm and we do not pretend to be one.

**Pricing badge** — invented social proof, on a site that elsewhere says it is new.
> Most makers pick this

> Unlimited labels
